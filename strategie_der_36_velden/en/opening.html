Earlier solutions

On November 5, 2009 Rein Halbersma published the first perft numbers for the 6×6 board (thread Perft for arbitrary board sizes: 5, 25, 106, 369, 1,301, 4,214, 12,912, 39,526, 113,614, 329,982, 933,564 up to depth 11); our move generator gives exactly the same numbers.

In October 2011 an alpha-beta search by Rein Halbersma from the start position, without databases and without a filter for graph-history interaction, suggested that the start position is a draw (scoring a repetition as a white win W35, as a black win L36). There was no proof of correctness yet. Shared privately with Ed Gilbert (October 19, 2011) and first published on April 8, 2017, on the World Draughts Forum (damforum.nl, formerly laatste.info) in the thread Search Algorithm.

Jan-Jaap van Horssen was the first to weakly solve 6×6 conclusively, in 2016, with proof-number search and the databases up to 8 pieces. He reported it on the forum on August 7, 2017 (thread Breakthrough Draughts), described it on August 13, 2017 in the thread Complexity of Checkers and Draughts on board sizes 6x6/…/14x14 and published it in the ICGA Journal in 2018.

Comparison with Van Horssen (2018)

Jan-Jaap van Horssen weakly solved 6×6 earlier, in Complexity of checkers and draughts on different board sizes, ICGA Journal 40 (2018) 341–352: a win/draw/loss database up to 8 pieces and proof-number search from the start position. Our databases and opening tree are independent of his, and agree with him on every point:

The difference at 8 pieces is exactly 84,825,312: the positions with 7 pieces against 1, which Van Horssen rightly leaves out, since each side starts with 6 pieces. He proves the value of the start position (without a transposition table, to avoid graph-history problems); here the distances to the end are exact for the whole tree up to ply 4.

Click a move to see the position on the large board; ◀ ▶ (or the arrow keys) step through the line. Values are for the side to move; for a move, for the side making it, with n the number of plies to the end of the game, the move included: W = win (odd), L = loss (even), D = draw. Blue = main line, green = side line, italics = continuation by the database, ? = a move that changes the result according to the database, with the better move or the refutation in [ ] (≡ = back to the position before that move), * = the only move that keeps the win or the draw. The # button turns the square names on the large board on or off; the button with the range of the square names (e.g. 1…18 or a1…f6 on 6×6) switches between numeric and algebraic notation, in the text as well.
