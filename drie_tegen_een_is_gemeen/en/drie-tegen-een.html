The colouring rule of the book is confirmed: from B = 9 on, for odd B only the colouring with playable corners (p1) wins. On B = 7 the wrong colouring still wins (5×9 p0, 7×13 p0) because the general method still reaches that far. How the families win is shown with the trapping positions.

The general method on narrow boards

  • B = 5, 6: every non-square board is won (checked up to 5×13 and 6×15). Three kings in a column close off the full width and can be pushed along the board; the longest win grows by about 2 plies per extra column.
  • B = 7: won up to 7×14 and 7×15 p1 (book: up to 7×11). From L = 16 on it is drawn. The longest wins jump from 25 to 35, 49, 53 and 67–73 plies: the space becomes tight.
  • B = 8: won at L = 9, 10, 11 and 15 (= 2B − 1), drawn at L = 12–14 and 16–19.
  • B ≥ 9: only the families remain.

Up to B = 8 the long diagonal decides; from B = 9 on three kings cannot win even when they hold it. With one long diagonal the condition amounts to "black does not hold it"; with two diagonals white really needs both.
