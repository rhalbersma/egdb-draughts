Traps on 8×7

3 against 2: four traps to break the fortress

On 8×8 the two double corners are 7 squares apart, and there the fortress always falls. On 9×8 they are 8 apart, and there the opposition decides. On 8×7 there are exactly two double corners, b1/a2 and b7/a6, both along the side of 7, and they are only 6 apart. (With playable instead of unplayable corner squares 8×7 is the mirror image, with the double corners along the h-file.)

With black to move and both black kings on the edge squares of their double corners (quiet positions) every position is lost at odd parity (3,172), and all but 4 at even parity (3,153 of 3,157). But in two different ways: at odd parity there are 8 traps, at even parity not a single one.

At odd parity white breaks the fortress with a trap, as on 8×8. There are 8 traps: the odd-numbered traps are active (with white to move white exchanges at once in trap 1: 1. c4-b3 a2×c4 2. d5×b3), the even-numbered ones passive (with white to move it is no trap). The board is symmetric under swapping top and bottom, and up to the mirror image there are 4 traps; all 4 are shown below.

Trap 1: white on c4, d5, e6, black on a2 and a6: 1. … a2-b1 2. c4-b5 a6×c4 3. d5×b3; 1. … a6-b7 2. c4-b3 a2×c4 3. d5×b3, and 2 against 1 wins. All moves of black: 1. … a2-b1 L30, 1. … a6-b7 L26, 1. … a6-b5 L12, 1. … a2-b3 L10.

Trap 2: white on b3, c4, d7, black on b1 and a6: 1. … b1-a2 2. c4-b5 a6×c4 3. b3×d5; 1. … a6-b7 2. d7-c6 b7×d5 3. c4×e6, and 2 against 1 wins. All moves of black: 1. … b1-a2 L32, 1. … a6-b7 L30, 1. … a6-b5 L14, 1. … b1-c2 L10.

Trap 3: white on a4, d5, e6, black on a2 and a6: 1. … a2-b1 2. a4-b5 a6×c4 3. d5×b3; 1. … a6-b7 2. a4-b3 a2×c4 3. d5×b3, and 2 against 1 wins. All moves of black: 1. … a2-b1 L30, 1. … a6-b7 L26, 1. … a6-b5 L12, 1. … a2-b3 L12.

Trap 4: white on b3, c4, d7, black on a2 and b7: 1. … b7-a6 2. c4-b5 a6×c4 3. b3×d5; 1. … a2-b1 2. d7-c6 b7×d5 3. c4×e6, and 2 against 1 wins. All moves of black: 1. … b7-a6 L32, 1. … a2-b1 L30, 1. … b7-c6 L6.

And with white to move? The active trap 1 forces an exchange at once with either side to move; with white to move white exchanges immediately: 1. c4-b3 a2×c4 2. d5×b3. The passive trap 2 gives an immediate exchange with black to move only. On 8×8 white then changes the tempo on the diagonal; on 9×8 that does not work along the edge, and on 8×7 it does not either. Still the passive trap with white to move is won here (W39).

All moves of white: 1. d7-c6 W39, 1. c4-d5 W43, 1. b3-a2 W43, 1. d7-e6 W47, 1. b3-a4 W47, 1. c4-d3 W51, 1. c4-b5 L32, 1. b3-c2 L12.

At even parity there is no trap at all. How white wins there, and in the passive trap with white to move, is on the next page: by expelling a black king from its double corner.
