# Strategie der 36 velden: checked with exact endgame databases

Herm Jan Brascamp, *Strategie der 36 velden* ("Strategy of the 36 squares",
[hijkendtc.nl](https://www.hijkendtc.nl/strategie-der-36-velden-hjb/)), 24 sections with more than 100 diagrams on
international draughts on the 6×6 board, systematically checked with exact databases. Every position was looked up or
computed to the end of the game: win, draw or loss, with the distance to the end in plies (DTM).

Brascamp wrote "dit werkje" ("this little work"), as he calls it, in the autumn of 2016, without a computer. In the
introduction he remarks: "A great advantage, of course, is that there is no draughts program for the 36-square board."
His subject is endgames with superior material: "White's strategy is aimed at getting three kings while keeping the
long diagonal. Black tries to conquer the long diagonal or to block the way of the white men." (Quotations translated
from Dutch.)

### History

Brascamp himself describes what his work builds on. In the introduction: "J.F. Moser wrote 'Strategie der honderd
velden' in 1966. In the 1990s Jannes van der Wal, Harm Wiersma and Hans Jansen analysed the game on the 6×6 board from
the start position." And about *De randslag-opening* ("the edge capture opening", chapter 7): "Around Christmas 2013 the
author, with hints from Hans Jansen, made a study of this opening. The conclusion then was that white would win with
3. 16-13. Recent research, as described in chapter 6 at diagram 6.9, has led to the new draw conclusion. Moreover, at
the end of 2013 it was taken as established that 3. 16-13 was the best move […]. Also wrong! Difficult, draughts on
the 6×6 board."

The databases settle both questions:

* **The conclusion of Christmas 2013 was right:** after 3. 16-13 white wins (53 plies). In *Zwaar manoeuvreren* (6.2)
  he writes: "Recently Hans Jansen and Herm Jan Brascamp have come to the conclusion that black (to move) draws in
  diagram 6.9 – so that after 2. … 2×9?? black still has the draw in hand. Or, reader, can you find a win?" The first
  part is right: diagram 6.9 with black to move is a draw. But the "so" does not hold. That "best play by both sides
  leads inevitably to diagram 6.9" he calls himself "a claim that will have to be proved there", and that claim is
  wrong: in the main line 10. 4-1 throws away the win, and 10. 12-8! wins. With it falls the conclusion of 7.7
  *Conclusie*, "that black can just reach a draw after the opening 1. 13-11 6-8 2. 11×6 2×9??".
* **"Also wrong!" is right:** 3. 16-13 is not the best move. 3. 15-12 (chapter 7.1, *Een offervariant*) wins faster,
  in 25 plies.
* As for the start position, analysed in the 1990s: it is a draw (see below).

### Measured against the databases

The analysis of the book stands almost everywhere:

* Of the 112 diagram claims ("white wins", "draw") 97 are right. Of the 15 that are not, 8 concern the positions at
  "white achieves nothing at all" in chapter 4 (*Een zwarte schijf op 15 en een zwarte dam*), whose placement of the
  pieces I derived from the text myself (4.11c/d, 4.12b/c). Real errors of the book: 7, in 7.3, 7.4, 7.6, 7.13, 7.22
  and 8.7 (with white and with black to move).
* Of the 333 variations 9 are wrong in substance. The 28 notation errors are typos whose meaning is almost always
  clear.
* The winning systems of *De lange lijn*, *Een hinderlijke schijf*, *Een zwarte schijf op 15 en een zwarte dam*, the
  Scouppe chapters and *Compositie op finesse 5* are almost entirely right, and so is *Het taaie eindspel* (chapter 9:
  only 2-7 wins, 49 plies, with the final finesse 21. 15-12!).
* The errors are almost all in *De randslag-opening* (chapter 7): positions with 8 to 10 pieces in which the win lies
  25 to 50 plies deep. At diagram 7.22 he writes "white wins on three of the four moves"; white also wins on the
  fourth, 7. … 7-10, but only with 10. 12-8!, followed by an endgame that is decided only after 39 plies. Lines like
  these can hardly be found without a computer.

This check is therefore above all a confirmation of his work. The refutations show where the databases see further in
those few places.
