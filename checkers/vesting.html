The fortress on larger boards

Opposition on 8×9

A king on the two edge squares of a double corner can never be captured and always has a waiting move. On 8×9 and 9×10 a black king in each double corner draws in half of the positions, and that half is decided by opposition: the parity (even or odd) of the sum of the rank numbers of all pieces flips with every move, and kings cannot lose a tempo, so with black to move the parity is fixed from the start.

The question came up in the thread Draughts on a 10x11 or 8x9 board on the World Draughts Forum (November 2026): do two kings in both double corners hold on 8×9, while they lose on 8×8? On 8×9 it depends on the opposition; why 8×8 is always lost is explained at the traps on 8×8.

Counted are the quiet positions (nobody can capture) with the black kings on the edge squares of two double corners: 8,228 and 8,244 on 8×9, 18,420 and 18,440 on 9×10, 37,030 and 37,050 on 10×11.

Black on a7 and i7 (shuttling with b8 and h8), white on c7, e7 and g7. With white to move (D) every move draws: black just shuttles a7-b8-a7. 1. c7-d8 D, 1. c7-b8 D, 1. c7-d6 D, 1. c7-b6 D, 1. e7-f8 D, 1. e7-d8 D, 1. e7-f6 D, 1. e7-d6 D, 1. g7-h8 D, 1. g7-f8 D, 1. g7-h6 D, 1. g7-f6 D.

With black to move black is in zugzwang and loses in 28 plies: 1. … a7-b8 2. g7-f8! b8×d6 3. e7×c5 and 2-1 wins: 3. … i7-h8 4. c5-d6 h8-i7 5. d6-e7 i7-h8 6. e7-f6 h8-i7 7. f8-g7 i7-h8 8. g7-h6 h8-i7 9. f6-g5 i7-h8 10. h6-i7 h8-g7 11. i7-h8 g7-f8 12. g5-f4 f8-e7 13. f4-e5 e7-f8 14. e5-d6 f8-g7 15. h8×f6+

With the white king on d6 instead of c7 (W29) white wins with 1. d6-c7!, reaching the same position with black to move.

Blue = main line, green = side line, italics = continuation by the database, ? = a move that changes the result according to the database, with the better move or the refutation in [ ] (≡ = back to the position before that move), * = the only move that keeps the win or the draw.
