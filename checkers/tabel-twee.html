The colour follows the fortress test: the black king on a square of a double corner, black to move, nobody can capture and black can avoid losing material at once, the white kings anywhere. Without a capture the parity of the sum of the rank numbers of all kings never changes, so the question is per parity: does one of those set-ups win for white, yes or no? 33 = yes at both parities: the fortress falls; the longest win takes 33 plies · 33* = yes at one parity: the opposition decides · (33) = at neither: the fortress holds · 31/13 = unplayable/playable corner squares; with playable ones on an odd × odd board there is no double corner and so no fortress. A board W × H is width × height, with W ≥ H.

  • 4×4 is the only board where the fortress holds: no set-up wins. White only wins when a white king stands on the other square of the double corner and the black king has to let itself be captured.
  • On the odd boards of height 3 with unplayable corners (5×3 to 11×3) the opposition decides. Every king move goes from an even to an odd rank or back, so nobody can lose a tempo, and on such a strip, where a king can only be captured on the middle rank, the outcome of every position with the black king in a double corner turns with the side to move. White on b3 and d3 and black on a2, for instance, is a draw with white to move and lost with black to move.
  • On all other boards the fortress falls at both parities, and nearly every set-up wins: only on 6×3, 5×4 and the boards of height 6 does black hold one to three.
