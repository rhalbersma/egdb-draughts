3 against 2 on 7×7

No escape to another double corner

On 8×7 white drives a black king out of its double corner, and there is no other double corner to flee to. On 7×7 there is: can the expelled king keep escaping to a next double corner, like the black king in 2 against 1 on 4×4?

On 7×7 with unplayable corner squares every corner is a double corner: b1/a2, f1/g2, a6/b7 and g6/f7. The double corners are 6 apart, on the same side and diagonally. Of the 28,276 quiet positions with black to move and both black kings on the edge squares of two double corners only 16 are drawn, and in those black has a shot.

The longest win without a capture for either side in the start position takes 85 plies. Black flees with both kings to the double corners along the g-file: after 13. … f3-g2 they stand on g6 and g2. White breaks the fortress by driving a black king out of its double corner: with 19. f3-g2! white occupies the free edge square next to the black king on f1, which leaves the corner (19. … f1-e2), and after 20. g2-f1 it cannot come back. It flees to the double corner b1/a2, but there white forces the decision with a sacrifice: 26. b3-c2! b1×d3 27. f7-g6 f5-e6 28. d5×f7, and 2 against 1 wins.

One might think that black could keep fleeing from double corner to double corner forever like this, as the black king does in 2 against 1 on 4×4, and that it is a draw. But 7×7 is not small enough for that: white still catches the expelled king in the second double corner. On 5×5 black does not manage either.
